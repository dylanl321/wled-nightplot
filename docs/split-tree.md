# Split tree

A split tree is a trunk that forks. The main trunk stands on the ground. At a split, two or more branches leave in different directions, and a branch can fork again. It is not the flat triangle (**Mini tree**), not a wrap around one post (**Spiral tree**), and not a line that splits.

You design it on the canvas. Drag a **circle** to move where the trunks meet. Drag a **diamond** to aim that tip. Drag the trunk itself to carry the branches that grow from it. **+** beside a tip forks that tip, and the other branches stay where they are. **Fork again** in the panel does the same for the selected tip. **Add branch** adds one more limb at a selected fork.

Each trunk has thickness. Lights wrap up it, the same idea as a spiral around a post. The coil stops short of a fork. A **ring** sits where the wraps meet, off to the side of the wood so they do not pile up on the crotch. Drag the ring, or type how far up and out it sits from the fork, to choose that junction.

A real string cannot jump to a sibling branch. After it finishes one trunk it takes a back path down the far side, then goes up the next. The knee of that back path is a dashed diamond: drag it, or type its height and how far out it sits, to choose where the string turns onto the next branch. If the two pieces do not meet by walking the tree, the drawing shows a dark jump.

The numbers stay in step with the drawing. Height, split height, and tip spacing are the whole tree, in the prop's unit (feet and inches, or metres). Each trunk also has its own height and how far out its end sits. Typing one of those moves the drawing; dragging updates the number. A string keeps its own LED product, its spacing in centimetres, and its length in metres.

**Even tips again** puts the tree back to an even Y from those whole-tree numbers.

The length stored on each run is the string length along that piece: the coil up a trunk, the short lead into a junction, or the back path down the far side. The height of a trunk is the height of its end, for a later effect that follows height.
